Dollar-Cost Averaging Basics

Personal view · Not financial advice

Personal view, not financial advice. This post is my own opinion and part of my learning journey. I'm not a financial professional. Please do your own research.

One of the first investing ideas that really clicked for me was dollar-cost averaging, often shortened to DCA. It's simple, and it takes a lot of the stress out of deciding when to buy.

What is dollar-cost averaging?

DCA means investing a fixed amount of money on a regular schedule, like every week or every month, no matter what the price is doing. Instead of trying to find the "perfect" moment, you spread your purchases out over time.

How it works in practice

Say you decide to put the same small amount into something every month. When the price is higher, that amount buys a little less. When the price is lower, it buys a little more. Over time, your average cost evens out across all those purchases.

Why it appeals to me

  • No market timing: I don't have to guess where the price is heading.
  • Less emotion: a set schedule keeps me from buying out of hype or freezing up out of fear.
  • Builds a habit: it turns investing into a routine, like paying a bill to my future self.
  • Works with small amounts: you don't need a big lump sum to get started.

The trade-offs

  • DCA doesn't guarantee a profit or protect against losses. If an asset goes down and stays down, you'll still lose money.
  • Investing a lump sum all at once can sometimes end up better, and sometimes worse. DCA is more about consistency and peace of mind.
  • Fees matter. Lots of small purchases can add up in fees on some platforms, so it's worth checking.

Getting started

If you're curious, the basic steps are: choose an amount you're comfortable with, pick a schedule, and stick with it. Many apps and exchanges offer recurring buys that automate this. Most importantly, only use money you won't need in the short term.
